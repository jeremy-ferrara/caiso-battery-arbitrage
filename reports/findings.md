# Findings: CAISO day-ahead battery arbitrage backtest

100 MW / 400 MWh battery, day-ahead hourly LMPs at the SP15 and NP15 trading hubs, Jul 1 2023 - Aug 31 2026 (38 months, 1,158 days per hub). Generated by `src/backtest.py` and `src/analysis.py`. All dollar figures are net profit after degradation unless stated.

## Setup (same for all strategies)

- 85% round-trip efficiency, $15/MWh degradation cost on MWh discharged, max 1 cycle (400 MWh discharged) per day.
- State of charge pinned to 50% (200 MWh) at every Pacific midnight. Each day is independent.
- **Perfect foresight (PF):** LP on the day's actual prices. Upper bound.
- **Rule-based:** one charge block and one discharge block per day, chosen from the average hour-of-day price profile over the trailing 30 days (excludes the current day). Settled at actual prices. Idles if the historical profile doesn't cover losses plus degradation.
- **Forecast-driven:** the same LP as PF, solved on a walk-forward price forecast for the day, then settled at actual prices. Forecast described below.
- **Persistence LP (diagnostic, not one of the three strategies):** the same LP solved on the previous day's actual prices, settled at the current day's. This is the most naive forecast and isolates what the ridge model adds.

**Comparison window.** Strategy comparisons use only dates where every strategy has a result: **Aug 7 2023 - Aug 31 2026, 1,121 days per hub.** The forecast needs 7 days of lags plus 30 days of training before its first prediction. Rule-based capture rates in earlier versions of this report used 1,151 days (from Jul 8 2023) and were 58.4% SP15 / 58.8% NP15. On the 1,121-day window they are 56.8% / 57.6%.

## Forecast model

- **Decision time.** Day-ahead bids for operating day D close around 10:00 PT on D-1. D-1's DA prices were published around 13:00 PT on D-2. The forecast for D therefore uses DA prices through D-1 only. No price from day D is used, and real-time prices are not used at all, because at bid time they are known for only part of D-1.
- **Features (per Pacific hour h of day D).**
  - Price at h on D-1 and on D-7.
  - Mean price at h over D-1 to D-7.
  - D-1's daily mean and max-min spread.
  - Day-of-week dummies.
  - Day of year encoded as sine and cosine. Month dummies were not used: an expanding window that starts in July has no training rows for most months during its first year.
- **No net load or solar data.** The project has only LMPs, so the model is price-only.
- **Model.** One ridge regression per hour of day (24 models), penalty 10 on standardized features. It is refit from scratch for each target day on all earlier days (expanding window), and standardization statistics come from the training rows only. The penalty and the feature list were set before seeing results and were not tuned.
- **Leakage test.** `tests/test_forecast.py` scrambles every price on and after day D and checks that D's forecast is unchanged.

## Things that look wrong or surprising

1. **The ridge forecast does not beat the naive persistence forecast, on accuracy or on profit.** Pooled over the period it has higher MAE (SP15 7.8 vs 7.3; NP15 6.7 vs 6.4), higher RMSE, and worse shape error. Dispatching on it earns less than dispatching on yesterday's prices: 87.2% vs 89.4% capture with both hubs pooled. It beats persistence on profit on only 44% (SP15) and 47% (NP15) of days. The ridge model does improve on persistence in two places:
   - Hours 5-15 at both hubs.
   - Calendar 2025 and 2026 on MAE (NP15 5.1 vs 5.5 in 2025), and on profit in those years (2025 both hubs 89.7% vs 88.9%; 2026 84.8% vs 82.9%).

   Ridge settings were not retuned after seeing this, because tuning on the backtest period would be lookahead. Persistence LP is effectively PF's schedule from the previous day replayed. It matches PF's mean discharge and idle-day count exactly.
2. **The ridge errors are concentrated at the evening peak and biased upward.**
   - At hours 17-20, ridge MAE is 1.2-1.7x persistence's.
   - Mean ridge error at hours 17-20 is positive every year, at both hubs: SP15 +7.4, +8.9, +4.6, +1.9 $/MWh for 2023-2026; NP15 +7.3, +5.1, +3.7, +1.9.
   - As a result the forecast overstates the daily spread: SP15 $65.7 forecast vs $55.3 actual, NP15 $46.4 vs $41.0.

   The LP trusts that inflated spread, so it trades more than PF and loses money more often. At SP15 it discharges 351 vs 320 MWh/day with 4 idle days vs PF's 66. At NP15 it loses money on 235 days vs 139 for persistence LP. A likely cause is the expanding window: it keeps 2023-24 evenings, when peaks were higher and spikier, including the Aug 15-16 2023 spike, while prices fell. The bias shrinking each year fits that explanation, but I have not tested it with a rolling window.
3. **Forecast accuracy and dispatch profit diverge in a specific way.** Ridge has worse MAE than persistence and still captures 91% of PF at SP15. All three forecasts have within-day rank correlations of 0.86-0.92. The LP mostly needs the order of hours (cheap midday, expensive evening), and every forecast gets that mostly right. Mean7 has the *best* rank correlation of the three and the worst MAE. It was not run through the LP.
4. **The forecast-driven strategy is weak at NP15 in winter and fall:** 44.7% (winter) and 59.6% (fall) capture, vs 63.3% and 75.4% for persistence LP. In Aug 7-Dec 2023 at NP15 it captured 64.8%, *less than the rule-based strategy* (66.5%). Those periods combine:
   - small NP15 spreads, where a few dollars of evening overstatement turns an idle day into a losing trade;
   - for 2023, a training set only weeks old that included the Aug 15-16 2023 price spike.
5. **Most of the gap between rule-based and forecast-driven is trading volume, not forecasting skill.** Both forecast LPs can do a full cycle a day; the rule can move at most half a cycle with SoC pinned at 50%. Against PF capped at the rule's volume, the rule captures 89.5% (SP15) and 79.5% (NP15). The forecast strategies capture 144% and 110% of that capped PF, which is above 100% because they aren't volume-limited, so that baseline doesn't apply to them.
6. **Arbitrage value fell steeply over the period.** SP15 PF went from $47.7/kW-yr (2024) to $31.3 (2025) to $19.4 (trailing 12 months). SP15 top-4/bottom-4 spread fell from $75 to $30/MWh (Jul-Dec 2023 vs 2025). Negative-price hours did not fall with the spread; Jan-Aug 2026 had about as many as Jan-Aug 2024. Lower, flatter evening peaks would explain this; not checked directly. Forecast-driven capture did not fall with spreads the way rule capture did (SP15 93.3% in 2024, 92.0% in 2025, 88.1% in 2026 Jan-Aug).
7. **Profit is concentrated in a few days.** Aug 15-16 2023 (evening DA peaks of $900-934/MWh on Aug 15 and $1,091-1,248/MWh on Aug 16 across the two hubs; checked in the raw data) contributed $510k of SP15 PF profit, 15% of the 2023 SP15 total. The top 10% of days produce 34% (SP15) and 45% (NP15) of PF profit, and 35% / 54% of forecast-driven profit. Aug 16 2023 is the best day for every strategy at both hubs. Both forecast LPs earned within $2k of PF that day; Aug 15 had the same evening spike shape, which the forecasts carried forward.
8. **SP15 earns about twice what NP15 does** under PF ($12.6M vs $6.0M over the full period), despite NP15's higher average price. SP15 has the wider daily spread and many more negative hours.
9. **The rule idles a lot at NP15 in winter:** 208 idle days at NP15 vs 25 at SP15, almost all in Q4/Q1. PF itself idles on 199 NP15 days, where no trade covers losses plus degradation, so many of these idle days are correct.
10. **Data quality:** no missing hours or duplicates in the DA series. Every Pacific day has 24 hours except the 6 DST-transition days (23 or 25). No day's rule or forecast-driven result exceeded PF.

## Not yet covered

- Real-time prices (the 15-min data is downloaded but unused).
- Net load, load forecast, or solar forecast features. CAISO publishes day-ahead load and renewable forecasts before the bid deadline, so they would be legitimate features, but they have not been pulled.
- A rolling training window, or a bias correction, to test the evening-overstatement explanation in item 2.
- Sensitivity to the degradation cost, the 50% midnight SoC pin, and the 30-day rule lookback.
