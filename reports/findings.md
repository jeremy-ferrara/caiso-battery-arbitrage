# Findings: CAISO day-ahead battery arbitrage backtest

100 MW / 400 MWh battery, day-ahead hourly LMPs at the SP15 and NP15 trading hubs, Jul 1 2023 - Aug 31 2026 (38 months, 1,158 days per hub). Generated by `src/backtest.py` and `src/analysis.py`. All dollar figures are net profit after degradation unless stated.

## Setup (same for both strategies)

- 85% round-trip efficiency, $15/MWh degradation cost on MWh discharged, max 1 cycle (400 MWh discharged) per day.
- State of charge pinned to 50% (200 MWh) at every Pacific midnight. Each day is independent.
- **Perfect foresight (PF):** LP on the day's actual prices. Upper bound.
- **Rule-based:** one charge block and one discharge block per day, chosen from the average hour-of-day price profile over the trailing 30 days (excludes the current day). Settled at actual prices. Idles if the historical profile doesn't cover losses plus degradation. No rule result for the first 7 days (Jul 1-7 2023), where there isn't enough history. Capture rates use only dates where both strategies have results (1,151 per hub).

## Things that look wrong or surprising

1. **About half the capture gap comes from the rule's structure, not its timing.** With SoC pinned at 50% at midnight, one charge block followed by one discharge block can move at most 200 MWh through storage per day (184 MWh delivered). PF has the same constraint but reaches a full cycle by discharging in the morning, recharging midday, and discharging again in the evening. PF delivers more than 184 MWh on 85% of SP15 days and 55% of NP15 days. Against a PF capped at the rule's daily volume, the rule captures **90.2% (SP15) and 80.2% (NP15)**, compared with 58% against unconstrained PF. The 58% headline therefore measures "naive schedule plus half the volume", not just "no foresight." A rule that also discharged in the morning, or a midnight SoC target below 50%, would change this number considerably. The forecast-driven strategy should be compared with both baselines.
2. **Arbitrage value fell steeply over the period.** SP15 PF went from $47.7/kW-yr (2024) to $31.3 (2025) to $19.4 (trailing 12 months). SP15 top-4/bottom-4 spread fell from $75 to $30/MWh (Jul-Dec 2023 vs 2025). Average prices fell too. Negative-price hours did not fall with the spread; Jan-Aug 2026 had about as many as Jan-Aug 2024. Lower and flatter evening peaks, rather than shallower midday troughs, would explain this. I have not checked that directly.
3. **Profit is concentrated in a few days.** Aug 15-16 2023 (evening DA prices above $1,000/MWh at both hubs; checked in the raw data, consistent across hubs) contributed $510k of SP15 PF profit, 15% of the 2023 SP15 total. The top 10% of days produce 34% (SP15) and 45% (NP15) of PF profit. Year-over-year comparisons involving 2023 depend heavily on this one event.
4. **SP15 earns about twice what NP15 does** under PF ($12.6M vs $6.0M), despite NP15's higher average price. SP15 has the wider daily spread and many more negative hours.
5. **The rule idles a lot at NP15 in winter:** 208 idle days at NP15 vs 25 at SP15, almost all in Q4/Q1. That is where NP15 winter capture of 28% comes from. On those days the trailing-30-day profile didn't clear the $15/MWh degradation cost. The rule also lost money on 137 NP15 days and 70 SP15 days (PF never loses money).
6. **Capture rate fell from ~68% to ~50%** between 2023 and 2025-26. As spreads shrink, a fixed degradation cost and fixed block timing take a larger share of the available value.
7. **Data quality:** no missing hours or duplicates in the DA series. Every Pacific day has 24 hours except the 6 DST-transition days (23 or 25). No day's rule result exceeded PF.
